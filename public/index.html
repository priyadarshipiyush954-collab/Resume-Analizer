    Architectural Rigor

    Engineered to simulate modern recruiter screening systems

    Modern applicant tracking systems (Workday, Greenhouse, Lever, Taleo) do not rely on simple word counts. They parse contextual hierarchies and measure technical relevance.

    01. Exact Keyword Density Matching

    Deterministic skill boundary tokenization

    Extracts and maps technical taxonomy using whole-word regex boundaries. Avoids false-positive matches (such as matching "Go" inside "Google") while correctly identifying multi-word competencies like "Machine Learning", "REST API", and "Data Analysis".

    02. Quantified Action Verbs

    High-impact impact scoring

    Scores active vs passive voice. Detects outcome-oriented verbs like Architected, Optimized, and Spearheaded that correlate with recruiter callback rates.

    03. Structural Readability

    Length & parsing hygiene

    Evaluates total word count against the standard 450–750 word recruiter baseline, ensuring your experience is neither under-documented nor diluted by filler content.

    04. Ephemeral Zero-Tracking Architecture

    100% in-memory processing with no storage

    Your resume PDF is parsed strictly within transient volatile memory buffers and destroyed immediately upon response completion. No files are saved to disk, no databases are queried, and no candidate data is retained.

    Editorial Best Practices

    Resume writing guides for high ATS conversion

    Practical, actionable frameworks to elevate your resume bullet points from passive duty lists to high-impact achievements.

    01. The Google XYZ Achievement Formula

    +

    The most effective resume bullet points follow Google's standard XYZ structure:

    "Accomplished [X], as measured by [Y], by doing [Z]"

    Weak Example: "Worked on backend microservices and improved database queries."
    Strong Example: "Decreased p99 database query latency by 38% [X], as measured by Datadog APM [Y], by restructuring indexing strategy and implementing Redis caching layers [Z]."

    02. Formatting for ATS Parsers (Avoid Formatting Traps)

    +

    Applicant Tracking Systems convert PDF files into raw linear text before parsing. Common design mistakes cause parsing failures:

    • Multi-column tables: Parsers often read across columns left-to-right rather than down, garbling dates and company names. Stick to clean single-column layouts.
    • Text in graphics or canvas: Any skills embedded inside graphical skill bars or icon meters cannot be extracted by ATS software.
    • Header/Footer contact info: Many legacy parsers completely ignore the Microsoft Word or PDF page header zone. Keep email and phone in the body.

    03. Grouping & Categorizing Skills Intentionally

    +

    Recruiters spend an average of 6 to 8 seconds scanning a resume. Rather than dumping an alphabet soup of 40 unorganized keywords into a single paragraph, categorize them logically:

    Languages: TypeScript, Python, SQL, Go
    Frameworks & Libraries: React, Node.js, Express, Next.js
    Cloud & Infrastructure: AWS (ECS, S3), Docker, Kubernetes, CI/CD
    Tools & Databases: PostgreSQL, Redis, Git, Linux
